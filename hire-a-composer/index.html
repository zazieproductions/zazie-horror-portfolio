IWho this is for

Commissions come from people who already know the atmosphere they want and need someone who can actually build it. The work is scored to picture, from the story outwards — not selected from a library and trimmed to length.

  • 02

  • 03

IIIHow a commission runs

Five stages, no surprises. The cheapest place to change your mind is stage two.

  1. Inquiry. You send the brief — format, runtime, tone, references, deadline, and what you can realistically spend. You get a real reply with questions or a first read, not a pitch deck. Open a scoring inquiry.
  2. Spotting. We watch the cut together or you send timecoded notes, and we decide where music earns its place — and where it should not. Spotting is where money is saved. How spotting works.
  3. Direction. A short sample cue or two, so you hear the world before the whole score is built. Nothing is committed to the full runtime until the direction is agreed.
  4. Score and design. Cues written to picture, revisions in rounds, sound design built in the same palette where the project wants it. Revision policy.
  5. Delivery. Full mix, stems, alternate and clean versions, timecode-matched cues, cue-sheet metadata — in the formats your edit and dub stage actually need. What delivery includes.

IVWhat lands in your edit

Every commission ships as a usable package, not a folder of rough bounces.

  • Stereo full mix of every cue, mastered for picture, with the dialogue sitting forward.
  • Stem families — dialogue-safe music, ambience and atmosphere, designed elements, low-end pressure — named and timecode-aligned.
  • Alternate and clean versions where the edit or the trailer needs a shortened, instrumental or stinger-only pass.
  • Timecode-matched cues against your picture-locked export, so nothing has to be nudged on the dub stage.
  • Cue sheet metadata — cue titles, durations, use type — prepared for the production's filing. Cue sheets and performing rights.

If your spec is unusual — surround, immersive, stems at 16+ families, a specific sample rate — say so at inquiry and it is written into the quote rather than discovered late.

VIWhat it costs

Not a flat fee, and not a mystery. A quote is built from your project's funding first, then shaped by scored runtime, cue density, live players, delivery complexity, timeline and rights. Student films start at $75.99; most other projects land in the few-hundred-dollar range, scaled to what the production actually has.

Read the full rate card and what moves the number, or the fees FAQ. If you have a number in mind, put it in the inquiry — a disclosed budget produces a usable answer rather than a guess.

VIIIQuestions before you commit

Do you only score horror?

Horror is the centre of gravity — psychological, folk, body, supernatural and cosmic — but the same palette scores thrillers, dark science fiction, experimental documentary and art-film work. Genre flexibility is part of the practice, not a compromise on it.

How long does a score take?

A short typically runs two to three weeks from spotting; a feature or a series episode four to eight weeks, depending on runtime and how much of it is scored. Rush scheduling is possible and is priced as such. What shapes the timeline.

Can you work to my temp track?

Yes — temp as a reference for function, not as a template to imitate. Send the temp and a sentence about what it is doing for you, and the score will do that job in its own language.

What do you need from me to quote?

Five things: format and runtime, a rough cut or scene list, tone references, your deadline, and the budget you can genuinely spend. Everything else can be worked out in the first reply. What to send.

Who owns the music?

The default grant for a commissioned score is a buyout on final payment, with authorship retained and credit written into the agreement; alternatives exist for festival, broadcast, album and sync use. The default grant · Licence options.

My budget is very small. Should I still write?

Yes. There is a student and micro-budget floor, a sliding scale tied to funding, and a licensing route that costs less than a commission. The honest answer to a small budget is a real scope, not silence. Student and low-budget work.
