III How to choose the right horror composer to hire

There is no single composer who fits every subgenre of horror. A retro 1980s slasher homage asks for a completely different harmonic language and instrument rack than an A24-style psychological drama, an Appalachian folk horror short, or a claustrophobic survival-horror game. Comparing scoring options across subgenre fit, picture sync, exclusivity, and budget prevents expensive mismatches in post-production:

V How a commission runs

The workflow is designed so independent directors, producers, and game leads always know what is being written, when they will hear it against picture, and what happens before final delivery. See the full five-stage scoring process for technical detail:

  1. 01 · Brief & written quote: Share your format, the music you need, your budget range and musical references via the inquiry form (screeners stay confidential; an NDA is available), and receive a written quote before anything is booked.
  2. 02 · Spotting session: We review the cut together to lock cue ins and outs, emotional shifts, intentional silence, and the sonic palette.
  3. 03 · Cues in picture context: Every cue is delivered synced to your video reference, so it is judged against picture and dialogue rather than in isolation.
  4. 04 · Revisions & delivery: The agreed revision rounds refine timing and mix, closing with 24-bit / 48 kHz WAV masters, cue-sheet metadata, and optional stems or game-engine loops.

VI How quoting works: your budget, your references

There is no published rate card at Zazie Productions, deliberately: a figure seen before you write anchors the conversation before your film is even described. Instead, the scoring inquiry asks for the budget range you have set aside for music and the scores you love, and the quote is built around your project, the music being commissioned, never your film's runtime. A 90-minute feature may need two scored minutes; a six-minute short may need nine.

  1. Tell me about the project: Format, how much music you need and your deadline, rough answers are fine, and only your name and email are required to start.
  2. Share your budget & references: Pick the budget range you have set aside for music and name the films, scores or tracks you love. The scope is then built around your production (not around a published rate card, because there isn’t one).
  3. Receive a written quote: A written, nonbinding quote follows review of your materials: scope, cue list, revision rounds, licence and fee, in that order. Nothing is booked until it is agreed in writing.

IX Frequently asked questions about hiring a horror composer

Direct answers to the practical questions filmmakers, producers, and game developers ask before commissioning a custom score. For 35 additional operational answers, see the full Horror Scoring FAQ.

01 Who is Zazie Kanwar-Torge, and what kind of horror projects does Zazie Productions score?

Zazie Kanwar-Torge is an IMDb-credited experimental psychological horror film composer for independent and arthouse cinema: also an experimental producer, sound designer, and multi-instrumentalist based in Asheville, North Carolina, operating through Zazie Productions LLC. He composes original custom scores for psychological, folk, and body horror, supernatural thrillers, dark sci-fi, experimental cinema, television series, and horror video games worldwide.

02 How much does it cost to hire a horror composer for a scene, short film, or feature?

Every Zazie Productions commission is quoted individually. There is no published rate card. In the inquiry you state the budget range you have set aside for music and the scores you love, whatever the format: a single scene, a complete short, or a substantial feature score. The written quote that follows weighs scored minutes of music (never film runtime), cue count, musical complexity, schedule, revision rounds and licence scope, and states the fee plainly before anything is booked. If a full original score does not fit the budget you share, a reduced scope or licensing existing material is suggested instead.

03 How do I choose the best horror composer to hire for my film or video game?

Evaluate four things before hiring any horror composer: first, subgenre alignment (for example, organic bowed metal, strings, and waterphone for psychological or folk dread versus retro analog synths for 1980s slasher homages); second, scored scenes under real dialogue rather than standalone tracks; third, verifiable screen credits on IMDb; and fourth, a written agreement specifying scored minutes, revision rounds, WAV/stem deliverables, and sync rights.

04 Do you compose music for horror video games as well as films and series?

Yes. Zazie Productions composes adaptive and linear horror game music including seamless exploration, tension, and chase loops, vertical intensity stems, stingers, and diegetic audio cues prepared for Wwise, FMOD, Unity, Unreal Engine, and Godot. Details on loop architecture and middleware hand-off are covered on the adaptive horror game scoring page.

05 Should I hire a custom horror composer or license royalty-free horror stock music?

Stock music works well for temp edits, game jams, and zero-budget prototypes because download packs are inexpensive and immediate. A custom horror score becomes essential for festival shorts, commercial indie games, trailers, and feature films because custom cues hit exact frame cuts, stay out of the frequency range of actors' dialogue, give your project an exclusive sonic identity no other film uses, and provide clean chain-of-title sync paperwork for distributors.

06 When in production or post-production should I reach out to a composer?

Reaching out during pre-production or assembly edit is ideal so themes and sonic palette can develop before temp-track lock sets in, while final cue timing is written once scenes reach picture lock. If your edit is already locked on a tight festival deadline, expedited (10–20 days) and rush (under 10 days) schedules are available subject to studio availability.

07 What audio files do you deliver, and how many revision rounds are included?

Every commission delivers master 24-bit / 48 kHz stereo WAV files synced to timecode, reference video check renders during review, and a complete cue list for cue sheets. The number of consolidated revision rounds included is stated in the written quote before composition begins. Multitrack stems, alternate mixes, and surround/Atmos deliverables can be added on request.

08 How do copyright ownership, festival rights, and buyouts work?

Standard commissions grant a Synchronization and Master Use license covering the named production and its festival/project release while the composer retains underlying musical copyright and writer's share for cue sheets. Expanded streaming/broadcast distribution, exclusivity windows, full work-for-hire copyright buyouts, and soundtrack album releases are all available by separate written agreement as detailed on the licensing and credits page.
