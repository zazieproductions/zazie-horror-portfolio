IWho this is for

Commissions come from people who know the atmosphere they need and want it built for their picture. The score is written to the story, cue by cue, not selected from a library and trimmed to length.

  • 01

    Directors and producers

    Psychological horror, folk horror, supernatural, surreal and experimental films: a score with themes, tension structure and room for silence.

  • 02

    Festival and art-film makers

    Short films and experimental work where music carries the argument. Feature-length scores are quoted individually.

  • 03

    Game teams

    Horror game scoring is a separate service with its own process. Read about game scoring.

IIWhat you can commission

The standard commission is original music composed to picture: themes, cues and tension structure, scored against your cut under an agreed scope. Sound design, adaptive music, trailer cuts and alternate mixes are specialist work. They are discussed after a feasibility check and quoted separately.

IIIHow a commission runs

  1. Inquiry. Send the format, scored minutes, the cue count if you know it, the delivery date, tone references, a private link to a rough cut and the budget you can spend. Request a Scoring Quote.
  2. Written quote. A nonbinding quote sets out the scope, the revision rounds and the licence. See what moves the quote.
  3. Spotting. We watch the cut together or work from timecoded notes, and decide where music earns its place. How spotting works.
  4. Composition. Cues are written to picture. Revisions are one consolidated round in Individual Scene and Focused Short-Film commissions, and two in Extended Short-Film, collected into one set of notes per round.
  5. Delivery. Stereo WAV files of the agreed cues, with a cue list and timings for your edit. What delivery includes.

IVWhat is included

Every commission includes the items below. Anything else is specialist work, quoted on request.

Included in every commission

  • Original music composed to picture for the cues agreed in writing.
  • Agreed scope confirmed in writing before composition begins: spotting notes, cue list and tone references.
  • Revision rounds as stated for your package: one consolidated round (Individual Scene and Focused Short-Film), two (Extended Short-Film).
  • Professional stereo WAV delivery (24-bit, 48 kHz unless your project specifies otherwise).
  • Cue titles and timings listed for your edit.

Specialist work, quoted on request

Discussed individually after a feasibility check. Not standard inclusions, and not part of a published estimate.

  • Multitrack or instrumental stems
  • Alternate, tension-variant or clean mixes
  • Surround or immersive mixes (5.1, 7.1, Dolby Atmos)
  • Final film mix, dialogue editing or dub-stage supervision
  • Sound design, foley and effects
  • Live musicians, soloists and session costs
  • Soundtrack album mastering and distribution
  • Adaptive or interactive music implementation for game engines
  • Trailer or teaser cut adaptation
  • Comprehensive audio post-production

VIWhat it costs

Packages are chosen by scored music minutes, not film runtime. Each package has a published range; the written quote is the only binding figure.

  • Individual Scene Scoring: From $250, for about 1–2 minutes of original music of original music.
  • Focused Short-Film Scoring: From $650, for up to 5 scored minutes of original music.
  • Extended Short-Film Scoring: From $1,500, for up to 12 scored minutes of original music.
  • Feature & Experimental Film Scoring: Custom quotation. Quoted after review of a cut.

Complexity, schedule, revisions, cue count and licence scope each change the figure. The rules are published on the services page, and the estimator there uses the same figures.

Preliminary estimate — final quotation subject to creative scope and written agreement. Indicative and nonbinding. Estimates come from the scope you enter. A written quote follows review of your cut or cue list, the schedule and the rights you need. Fees are in US dollars and exclude taxes, live players and third-party clearances.

VIINot ready for a custom score?

If the deadline is very short, or you need one existing track rather than a score, licensing existing material can be the better route. Licence terms are set out in writing, not negotiated from the description on this page. Licensing terms are under review, so read them before you accept anything.

VIIIQuestions before you commit

Do you only score horror?

Horror is the centre of the practice: psychological, folk, body, supernatural and cosmic. The same palette also scores dark thrillers, dark science fiction, experimental documentary and art-film work.

How long does a score take?

Schedules are agreed per project. The estimator offers three schedule options. Work due in under ten days is priced as rush and confirmed in writing before a quote is accepted.

Can you work to my temp track?

Yes. Send the temp and a sentence about what it does for the scene. The score will do that job in its own language, and it will not imitate the temp.

What do you need from me to quote?

Format, scored minutes, a rough cut or scene list, tone references, your delivery date and the budget you can spend. A disclosed budget gets a usable answer rather than a guess.

Who owns the music?

Composition and the rights in your project are separate. The published price includes the standard project licence: non-exclusive use of the delivered cues in the named production, including its festival run, its own release and its promotional use. Expanded distribution, exclusivity, a copyright assignment (buyout) and a soundtrack release are never added automatically; each needs a separate written agreement and is quoted individually. The rights in each commission are stated in the written quote.

My budget is small. Should I still write?

Yes. Independent and micro-budget productions are welcome to inquire. Where appropriate, a reduced musical scope or licensing of existing material may offer a more accessible alternative. The lowest starting fee is $250, for Individual Scene Scoring. A smaller scope costs less, because the quote follows scored minutes, cue count and complexity. A lower budget never automatically includes unlimited music, unlimited revisions or any rights beyond the standard project licence.
