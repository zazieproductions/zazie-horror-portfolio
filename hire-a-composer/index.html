IWho this is for

Commissions come from people who know the atmosphere they need — and want it built for their picture.

The score is written to the story, cue by cue, not selected from a library and trimmed to length.

  • 01

    Directors and producers

    Psychological horror, folk horror, supernatural, surreal and experimental films: a score with themes, tension structure and room for silence.

  • 02

    Festival and art-film makers

    Short films and experimental work where music carries the argument. Substantial feature scores are quoted individually; a single scene/cue for a feature can use Individual Scene Scoring.

  • 03

    Game teams

    Horror game scoring is a separate service with its own process. Read about game scoring.

IIWhat you can commission

The standard commission is original music composed to picture: themes, cues and tension structure, scored against your cut under an agreed scope. Sound design, adaptive music, trailer cuts and alternate mixes are specialist work. They are discussed after a feasibility check and quoted separately.

IIIHow a commission runs

  1. Inquiry. Start with what you are making and what you need. If known, add scored minutes, a target date and tone references; budget and a rough-cut link are optional and can follow later. Request a scoring quote.
  2. Written quote. A nonbinding quote sets out the scope, the revision rounds and the licence. See what moves the quote.
  3. Spotting. We watch the cut together or work from timecoded notes, and decide where music earns its place. How spotting works.
  4. Composition. Cues are written to picture. Each cue gets two defined rounds of notes, collected into one set of notes per round.
  5. Delivery. Stereo WAV files of the agreed cues, with a cue list and timings for your edit. What delivery includes.

IVWhat is included

Every commission includes the items below. Anything else is specialist work, quoted on request.

Included in every commission

  • Original music composed to picture for the cues agreed in writing.
  • Creative scope confirmed in writing before composition begins: spotting notes, cue list and tone references.
  • Two defined revision rounds per cue, collected into one set of notes per round.
  • Professional stereo WAV delivery (24-bit, 48 kHz unless your project specifies otherwise).
  • Cue titles and timings listed for your edit.

Specialist work, quoted on request

Discussed individually after a feasibility check. Not standard inclusions, and not part of a published estimate.

  • Instrumental stems by family
  • Alternate, tension-variant or clean mixes
  • Surround or immersive mixes (5.1, 7.1, Dolby Atmos)
  • Final film mix, dialogue editing or dub-stage supervision
  • Sound design, foley and effects
  • Live musicians, soloists and session costs
  • Soundtrack album mastering and distribution
  • Adaptive or interactive music implementation for game engines
  • Trailer or teaser cut adaptation

VIWhat it costs

Packages are chosen by scored music minutes, not film runtime. The listed fees are starting fees in USD; the scope bands do not overlap, and the written quote confirms the agreed scope and figure.

  • Individual Scene Scoring: From $250 starting fee, for Approximately 1–2 minutes of original music.
  • Focused Short-Film Score: From $650 starting fee, for More than 2 and up to 5 scored minutes.
  • Extended Short-Film Score: From $1,500 starting fee, for More than 5 and up to 12 scored minutes.
  • Feature Film & Experimental Cinema: Custom quotation, with an indicative minimum engagement of $2,500 for substantial scoring projects. A single feature scene/cue may use Individual Scene Scoring and is not subject to this minimum.

Complexity, schedule, revisions, cue count and licence scope each change the figure. The rules are published on the services page, and the estimator there uses the same figures.

All fees are in USD. Published fees are starting fees, not fixed-price caps or a guarantee of a specific amount of finished music; scope labels are guides. Estimates are indicative and nonbinding. A written quote follows review of your cut or cue list, musical complexity, cue count, schedule, revisions and licence. Substantial feature-film and experimental-cinema scoring engagements have an indicative minimum of $2,500; this minimum does not apply to an individual scene/cue commission for a feature. Taxes, live players and third-party clearances are additional.

VIINot ready for a custom score?

If the deadline is very short, or you need one existing track rather than a score, licensing existing material can be the better route. Licence terms are set out in writing, not negotiated from the description on this page. Licensing terms are under review, so read them before you accept anything.

VIIIQuestions before you commit

01Do you only score horror?

Horror is the centre of the practice: psychological, folk, body, supernatural and cosmic. The same palette also scores dark thrillers, dark science fiction, experimental documentary and art-film work.

02How long does a score take?

Schedules are agreed per project; the estimator offers three schedule options. Work due in under ten days is priced as rush and confirmed in writing before a quote is accepted.

03Can you work to my temp track?

Yes. Send the temp and a sentence about what it does for the scene. The score will do that job in its own language, and it will not imitate the temp.

04What do you need from me to quote?

Start with a format and a brief description of the project. Scored minutes, a scene list or rough cut, tone references and a target date help when available; sharing a budget is optional and can help tailor scope.

05Who owns the music?

Composition and the rights in your project are separate. The base price covers a non-exclusive project licence. A full buyout (transfer of copyright), exclusivity and soundtrack rights are quoted separately and are never added automatically; the rights in each commission are stated in the written quote. See licensing, rights and credits.

06My budget is small. Should I still write?

Yes. Individual Scene Scoring starts at $250 USD — a starting fee, not a fixed-price cap or a promise of a set duration. The $2,500 indicative minimum applies only to substantial feature/experimental scores, never to a single scene or cue within a feature. If a commission does not fit, licensing existing material may.
