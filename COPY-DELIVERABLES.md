**Heading**

3. **Deliver cleanly** — Spotting notes, stems, alternates, and an organized handoff.

## 5. A/B test plan

Run one test at a time. Keep the reel, pricing, form, traffic source, and page layout constant so the copy is the variable.

### Test A — Hero positioning

**Hypothesis:** Naming the service and mechanism immediately will create more qualified action than leading with an anti-temp warning.

- **Variant A — current:** “Original horror scores. Written to picture.”
- **Variant B:** “Original scores for psychological horror.”
  - Subhead: “Themes, tension, and atmosphere shaped around your edit—from first spotting session to final stems.”

**Primary metric:** scoring-inquiry starts per unique session.

**Secondary metrics:** reel-play starts, services-section visits, completed inquiries, and qualified inquiry rate. Do not use raw page time as a success metric.

### Test B — CTA specificity

**Hypothesis:** A project-specific CTA will reduce the perceived commitment of the first click and increase qualified inquiries.

- **Variant A — current:** “Discuss your project”
- **Variant B:** “Start a scoring inquiry”

**Primary metric:** `mailto` click-through rate from the hero and contact section.

**Guardrail:** percentage of inquiries containing project type, runtime, budget, and deadline. A click lift without better inquiry detail is not a win.

### Test C — Price framing

**Hypothesis:** A firm $150 minimum will filter out non-buyers without materially reducing qualified micro-budget inquiries.

- **Variant A — current:** “Micro / student from $150”
- **Variant B:** “Micro / student score from $150 · scope set to runtime and cue count”

**Primary metric:** qualified inquiries per services-section visit.

**Secondary metrics:** micro-budget inquiry rate, average quoted project value, and inquiry-to-call rate.

**Guardrail:** never hide the minimum after a click. The form and estimator must show the same $150 floor.

### Test D — Proof placement

**Hypothesis:** A verified collaborator rating near the hero will reduce risk better than a scrolling list of genre labels.

- **Variant A — current:** the proof bar with IMDb, Crystal Fox Films, festival shorts, and genre range.
- **Variant B:** a compact proof block: “5.0 collaborator rating · 4 featured reviews · Reply within two business days.”

**Primary metric:** inquiry starts per session.

**Guardrail:** only use the review count, rating, credits, and response standard if they remain accurate and verifiable.

### Sample-size logic

Use a two-sided two-proportion test for the primary conversion event. For an initial planning estimate:

```text
n per variant ≈ 2 × (zα/2 + zβ)² × p̄(1 − p̄) / (pB − pA)²
```

For a 3.0% baseline inquiry-start rate and a minimum detectable lift to 4.5% (a 1.5 percentage-point absolute increase), with 95% confidence and 80% power, plan for roughly **2,500 sessions per variant**. If the site cannot reach that volume, do not call a small result conclusive: run until at least 100 qualified inquiries across the test, use the direction of secondary metrics, and repeat the winner against a new challenger.

Do not stop when one variant has a few more clicks. Pre-register the primary metric, minimum sample, test window, and guardrail before launch. Report both conversion rate and qualified inquiry rate.
