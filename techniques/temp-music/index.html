The Dread Codex · Essay 01

Temp love: when the edit falls for music it can't keep

Every cut gets scored twice — once in secret, by the editor, with someone else's music. This is the psychology of that affair and how to end it.

Essay · ~7 min Written by Zazie Kanwar-Torge September 2026

Temp music is the placeholder score cut into a film during editing — borrowed cues used to set pacing, mood, and rhythm before original music exists. It is a legitimate tool. Nearly every edit in history has used it.

Temp love is what happens next, and it is the most expensive emotion in post-production: the moment the edit — and everyone who has watched the edit — falls for that borrowed music and stops being able to imagine the film without it.

Temp got you through the edit. It will not carry the room. The temp track is rented feeling, and rented feeling does not get your film remembered. — the first law of temp

01 · The mechanism

Why temp love happens (it's not weakness)

The mechanism is a documented effect in psychology: mere exposure — human beings prefer things they have encountered before, even when they can't remember encountering them. An editor lives with a temp cue for three hundred plays. The director watches forty cuts. By picture lock, the temp isn't a placeholder to anyone in the building; it is the film's music, and every alternative sounds like a stranger touching the cut.

Meanwhile, something quieter and worse has happened: the edit's rhythm has absorbed the temp's rhythm. Cuts land on the temp's beats. Scenes breathe at the temp's tempo. The film has been physically shaped around music it doesn't own — which is why replacing temp late feels less like a swap and more like surgery.

Temp love is not a taste failure. It is exposure plus structure. Which means it can only be beaten with a counter-structure, not with willpower.

02 · The bill

What temp love costs

  • The replacement shock. The first screening with original score reads as "wrong" to everyone conditioned by the temp — not because the score is weaker, but because it's new. Notes come in like grief. Weeks burn.
  • The budget leak. The longer temp survives into post, the more the mix, the sound design, and the marketing cut lean on it, and the more expensive every mile of divergence becomes.
  • The clearance trap. When the spell holds, productions sometimes try to license the temp itself. Festival darling temp cues can cost more than an entire original score — or simply be unavailable at any price.
  • The identity loss. The subtle one: a film scored in the grammar of its temp is scored in the grammar of every other film that used the same cues. It arrives sounding like a compilation of other people's instincts.

None of this is an argument against temp music. It's an argument against letting it rewrite the film.

03 · The counter-structure

Four moves that break the spell

  1. Bring the composer in before picture lock. The single highest-leverage move in film music. Original music arriving while the cut can still breathe means the edit forms around music the film actually owns. Every week earlier is compounding.
  2. Hold a real spotting session. Not an email thread. A session where composer and director decide, cue by cue, where music enters, where it refuses to enter, and what each cue is allowed to know. Half of psychological horror scoring is choosing where the score stays silent.
  3. Demand one demo cue on your footage. Not a portfolio piece — your cut, with your faces and your air. This is the only proof a composer can actually score your film, and it starts breaking the temp's spell immediately, because the demo is new and yours.
  4. Ask for temp inversion. The professional counter to temp love: keep the temp's function — where it builds, where it releases, what it withholds — and replace its identity: different engine, different pulse, different promise. The audience keeps the shape they were trained on; the film gets a voice it owns.

That last move is standard practice in how this studio works: write away from the temp's grammar so the original score reads as inevitable, not imitative.

04 · FAQ

Asked in every spotting session

What is temp music?

Placeholder music cut into a film during editing to set pacing and mood before the original score exists. A tool for rhythm and tone — not a score, and rarely clearable at indie budget.

What is temp love?

The point where the edit — and everyone who has watched it — becomes attached to the temp. Mere exposure makes the temp feel inevitable after hundreds of viewings, the edit's rhythm absorbs its rhythm, and any replacement sounds wrong by comparison.

How do you break it?

Composer in before picture lock, a real spotting session, one demo cue on your footage, and temp inversion — keeping the temp's function while replacing its identity.
