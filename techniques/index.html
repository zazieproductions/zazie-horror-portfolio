02 · The Lexicon of Dread

Ten terms, defined from the bench

Unnamed technique can't be briefed, spotted, or repeated on deadline. These are the working terms — some inherited from the craft, some coined in this studio — with the definitions used on actual spotting sessions. The extended essays behind each technique live here.

Temp love
The edit's emotional attachment to temporary music after repeated exposure — the most expensive emotion in post-production. Full essay
Temp inversion
Writing away from the temp track's grammar: keeping its function — where it builds, where it withholds — while replacing its identity, so the original score reads as inevitable rather than imitative.
The uncanny interval
Harmonic wrongness compressed into a single interval — the tritone, the minor second — placed where the ear expects resolution. The cheapest unit of dread ever discovered.
The long inhale
A multi-minute crescendo that structurally refuses to resolve. The audience breathes in with the cue and never gets to exhale.
Negative-space scoring
The deliberate removal of music at the exact moment the scene expects relief. The audience has been trained that music means danger; when it stops and nothing happens, the danger moves inside them.
Dissociative harmony
Consonant, even beautiful material placed in the wrong register, wrong instrument, or wrong scene — beauty that reads as wrongness. The sound of a mind detaching.
Sub-frequency dread
Felt-not-heard drone beds mixed under dialogue — infrasound-adjacent pressure the body registers as physical unease before the ear registers anything at all.
The vanishing leitmotif
A character theme that degrades with each statement — notes removed, tuning slipping, orchestration thinning — so recognition itself becomes unreliable, the way memory does.
Diegetic blurring
Source music inside the story — radio, music box, tape — that curdles seamlessly into underscore, dissolving the line between the character's world and the score. Retroactively poisons every cue before it.
Psychophonic bed
A sustained textural underscore built from processed acoustic sources — scraped, bowed, and struck materials — that behaves psychologically rather than melodically. The studio's term for the layer that lives under everything. Hear: “Scraping Psychophonic Bed” on the reel
