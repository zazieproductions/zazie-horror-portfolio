## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 The strategic thesis in five sentences

1. The site currently competes in the **generic "horror composer for hire" bracket**, where the SERP is Fiverr, itch.io, SoundBetter, Reddit and faceted marketplace pages — a bracket that competes on price (gigs start at $20–45) and that a $250-floor specialist studio cannot win by sounding like the marketplace.
2. The four terms the owner wants to own — **psychological horror, folk horror, body horror, experimental art-house film scoring** — have a completely different SERP: editorial listicles, Wikipedia, soundtrack retailers and interviews with *famous* composers, but **no working composer's service page that owns any of the four phrases**. The niches are authoritative in tone but commercially uncontested.
3. The site already holds the raw materials of authority — IMDb-verified credits, an Ars Electronica 2026 spatial-audio selection, EXiS Seoul, MUSLAB/Cero, a Black Mountain College Museum commission, 9 production records, a 30-cue showreel, 24 catalogue works, published pricing, a strict no-overclaiming credit discipline, `llms.txt`/`llms-full.txt`, and an explicit allowlist for every major AI crawler. Most competing composers have none of this infrastructure.
4. The decisive move is therefore not more generic SEO: it is to **build four subgenre authority pillars (commercial + editorial hybrid pages), an answer-first journal, and a music-supervisor-ready press/reel layer**, shifting the site's vocabulary from "affordable horror composer" to "the specialist studio for psychological and art-house horror" while keeping rate transparency as a trust asset below the fold.
5. A24's own Music Supervisor job description states the job includes to **"collect and curate Composer reels to present to internal teams and filmmakers."** Reels are the currency of this market. The programme below is designed so that when a supervisor, director or AI answer engine goes looking for a composer in these four lanes, Zazie Kanwar-Torge is the best-documented, most clearly categorised, most citable option in the results.

### 1.2 The ten findings that matter

1. **"Art house" / "arthouse" appears zero times on the entire site.** "Elevated horror" also appears zero times. The owner's stated number-one positioning term has no on-page footprint at all — not in a title, H-tag, body, schema or `llms.txt`. (Evidence: recursive grep across all served HTML and TS sources, 0 matches.)
2. **Only one page title carries the phrase "psychological horror composer"** (`/composer`). There is no page whose job is to rank for *psychological horror composer*, *folk horror composer*, *body horror composer* or *experimental/art-house film composer* as a commercial query.
3. **Subgenre coverage is unbalanced at the file level.** "Psychological horror" appears in 116 files and "body horror" in 109 (largely cue-page schema and categories), but "folk horror" appears in only 24, and the art-house vocabulary is absent. Body-horror strength is real but trapped in item pages with no hub to concentrate it.
4. **The generic head terms are a marketplace SERP; the subgenre terms are an editorial SERP.** Sampling "psychological horror film composer for hire" returns Reddit/itch.io/Fiverr threads; sampling the subgenre craft terms returns BFI, Pitchfork, Scientific American, Bandcamp Daily, Wikipedia and listicles. The first is winnable mid-tail; the second is an *authority vacuum* a practitioner can occupy with sourced, answer-first content.
5. **The named authorities in these niches are editorial entities, not local SEO competitors.** Mica Levi (*Under the Skin*), Colin Stetson (*Hereditary*, *The Menu*), Bobby Krlic / The Haxan Cloak (*Midsommar*), Mark Korven (*The Witch*, *The Lighthouse*), Jóhann Jóhannsson (*Mandy*), Ben Frost, Cristobal Tapia de Veer (*Smile*), Howard Shore (Cronenberg's body horror), Robert Aiki Aubrey Lowe (*Candyman*), Disasterpeace (*It Follows*), Michael Abels (*Get Out*). They own listicles, not commercial SERPs — and their existence gives the journal programme a legitimate, citable lineage with no affiliation claims.
6. **"Art horror / arthouse horror" is the canonical encyclopaedic term** (the Wikipedia article is titled *Art horror*, with "elevated horror" as an alias; A24 is credited with popularising the category). "Elevated horror" remains high-volume in search despite being contested by filmmakers (Aster, Peele, Carpenter). The pillar must use all three variants — *art-house horror*, *art horror*, *elevated horror* — and explain the debate rather than pick one label.
7. **The GEO infrastructure is ahead of ~98% of competitors but is under-used.** All AI crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot/Claude-Web, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Cohere, DuckAssist) are explicitly allowed, and both llms.txt files are generated from the typed data source. 2026 studies find llms.txt alone has negligible citation impact (Google states it does not use it); the citation drivers are answer-first structure, statistics, outbound citations, freshness, speed and entity density — where the current hub pages are only partially optimised.
8. **The site's most differentiated commercial asset — published, honest pricing — is also its biggest positioning risk.** Leading pillar pages with "from $250" reinforces the marketplace bracket the site is trying to leave. Pricing should remain (transparency is rare and converts, and supervisors respect budget clarity) but sit *after* craft, credentials and subgenre fit on the new authority pages.
9. **There is no press room and no supervisor pack.** Press coverage exists (Billboard Wire, Grammy Weekly, Limitless Magazine, Visual Container, BMC) but is scattered across the homepage and `/composer`. A24-class buyers and journalists need one page with a downloadable one-sheet, subgenre-curated reels, verified credits, cue-sheet/stem/deliverable readiness, and broadcast-ready facts. `/press` does not exist.
10. **Measurement is still blind.** The zero-tracking stance is a defensible privacy claim and must not be broken with third-party analytics. But Google Search Console is server-side, cookieless and free; without it, none of this programme can be validated. Cloudflare server logs additionally expose AI-crawler fetches (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot), which typically precede citations by 24–72 hours. GSC verification plus log monitoring is the highest-leverage zero-privacy-cost change available.

No practitioner can guarantee a number-one ranking, and this dossier does not promise one. What the evidence supports is a defensible path to **top-three visibility for the subgenre commercial phrases and featured/AI-answer ownership for the craft questions**, because:

- the content gap is specific and buildable: four pillars, eight journal articles, one press layer — a finite, specified programme in §9–12, not an open-ended "make more content" suggestion.

## 4. The buyer: how A24, NEON and IFC Midnight actually hire composers

### 4.1 Evidence on the process

- **A24 Music Supervisor job description (public posting, 2025):** responsibilities include reading development scripts and building music budgets; **"collect and curate Composer reels to present to internal teams and filmmakers"**; collaborating on composer hires and licensing documentation; managing the note process between filmmakers, creatives and composer; steering cue-sheet and licensing curation. A separate A24 clearance role references maintaining "the external-facing audio catalog" and sourcing "playlists, and composer reels for the Music Supervisor and filmmakers" across 27+ active projects.
- **A24 Music (in-house label since 2021, Secretly Distribution):** Billboard documents a deliberate strategy of *establishing new talent under its own banner rather than recruiting established names* (Vulture). The New Yorker describes lasting auteur relationships and democratic, small-group creative decisions.
- **Discovery channels:** festival premieres (Sundance, Cannes, TIFF, SXSW — ScriptMatch's 2026 A24 sourcing analysis), agency/legal relationships, and online tracking of emerging creative communities. A24's mandate explicitly favours "liminal horror and elevated genre: unconventional, psychologically grounded, or formally inventive approaches rather than slasher or broad supernatural fare."
- **Indie hiring norm (vi-control, Quora, documentary.org):** the director usually initiates the composer choice; the music supervisor recommends and shortlists when the director has no name; reels, referrals and festival work drive the shortlist; cue sheets, stems and rights paperwork gate the distribution stage.
- **IFC Midnight precedent:** Ronen Landa (*At the Devil's Door*, *The Pact*) was hired after director Nicholas McCarthy encountered his *experimental concert music* — evidence that art-music/experimental credentials are a direct hiring channel in this lane, not a vanity credential.
- **NEON's lane (ScreenRant classification + title evidence):** body horror (*Titane*), psychological dread (*The Lodge*), folk/experimental (*In the Earth*), horror satire — near-perfect overlap with the four pillars. NEON's composers (e.g., Cliff Martinez on *The Neon Demon*) are chosen for distinctive, director-aligned voices.
- **Distribution-stage requirements (thoolie.com, documentary.org, ASCAP):** final mixes, stems, alternates, session files, completed cue sheet, metadata to distributor spec, chain of title, work-for-hire or licence paperwork, E&O readiness. Missing paperwork stalls deals; supervisors filter for composers who will not create clearance risk.

### 4.2 What this means for the site (the "supervisor test")

When a supervisor or director opens the site during a 20-minute shortlisting pass, they need to answer five questions within three clicks:

1. **Does this composer do *my exact subgenre*?** → four pillars, each naming the lane in title/H1/first sentence, with subgenre-curated cues.
2. **Is the work actually good and picture-tested?** → film samples in context, verified credits with IMDb links, festival/institution seals.
3. **Will they be administratively safe?** → a deliverables/rights section naming stems (24-bit/48 kHz WAV), cue sheets, PRO registration support, buyout/default rights, NDA willingness — content already spread across `/process`, `/licensing`, `/faq`, `/terms`, assembled in the supervisor pack.
4. **Can I forward this to my filmmaker?** → one-sheet PDF/PRESS page, private reel options (ReelCrafter is already linked), quote-ready bio and boilerplate.
5. **Are they a distinctive *voice*, not a generic vendor?** → the experimental/art-house pillar, instrument-builder narrative, spatial-audio work, curated journal — the things a marketplace gig cannot imitate.

### 4.3 Positioning rules for distributor-adjacent copy

- Name the lane, never the relationship. The existing reference-topic pattern (`HORROR_REFERENCE_TOPICS` in `entities.ts`, with explicit "not an affiliation or production credit" language) is exactly right and must be preserved. No copy may imply A24/NEON/IFC work or endorsement.
- Use buyer vocabulary truthfully: "the art-house / elevated-horror lane associated with distributors such as A24, NEON and IFC Midnight" is factual market context; "A24-style" should stay rare and always framed as a reference language (the `/hire-a-composer` page already does this once, well).
- Lead with voice and credentials; price only after fit. Supervisors expect budget questions, but a $250-first impression signals student-film tier. The pillars target *commissioning fit*; `/services` remains the authoritative answer for cost.
- The experimental credentials are the differentiator. Ars Electronica + BMC + EXiS + MUSLAB is a profile closer to the Mica Levi/Ben Frost art-music pipeline than to a gig freelancer — the press release and pillar copy should make that identity explicit without inflating credits.

## 6. AEO strategy (answer engines, People-Also-Ask, featured snippets)

Google's FAQ rich-result restriction (2023) means FAQ schema rarely renders visually now — but answer-engine optimisation is still decisive because the same structures feed AI Overviews, AI Mode and third-party LLMs.

### 6.1 The answer-first content standard (apply to every new page)

1. **Answer capsule.** A standalone 40–60 word paragraph directly under the H1 that defines the page's core answer, names the entity (Zazie Kanwar-Torge / Zazie Productions), includes the primary keyword and at least one hard number (cue count, fee, channel count, year). 2026 GEO research (Princeton KDD study; SparkToro: 44% of LLM citations come from the first 30% of page content) makes this the single highest-lifting structural element.
2. **Question-shaped H2s.** Every section heading phrased as the buyer's actual question ("What does a psychological horror score cost?", "How is folk horror music different from other horror scores?"). Vague headings ("Our Philosophy") are banned on authority pages.
3. **40–80 word definitional blocks** under each question heading — clean, quotable units an engine can lift verbatim.
4. **Three-plus lists and one comparison table per pillar** (GEO formatting-density finding; tables show ~34% citation lift in Erlin's 2026 trend data).
5. **Statistics and quotations with outbound citations.** Princeton: statistics up to +40%, citations/quotations up to +41% generative visibility. Cite real sources: the University of Zurich horror-music study, Scientific American, BFI, Wikipedia *Art horror* / *Folk horror*, Princeton GEO study itself, ASCAP/BMI documentation. Outbound authority is symmetric: pages that cite trustworthy sources get treated as trustworthy.
6. **Named-entity density.** Name specific instruments, films, composers, techniques, standards (24-bit/48 kHz WAV, Wwise/FMOD, PRO, cue sheet). Retrieval models favour specificity.
7. **FAQ block of 8–12 genuine questions** per pillar, marked up with FAQPage JSON-LD (already the site's pattern), each answer self-contained and linking deeper.
8. **Freshness.** Content updated within three months earns roughly double the ChatGPT citations of stale content in SE Ranking's 216,524-page study. Every pillar and journal article carries a visible reviewed date; the quarterly review cadence is in §13.

## 13. Risks and guardrails

1. **No affiliation inflation.** A24/NEON/IFC and named composers are reference topics only. The existing schema/description pattern must be reused verbatim in spirit; any implied endorsement is both a trust and legal risk.
2. **No invented facts.** This dossier introduces no credits, awards, statistics or testimonials. All statistics in future journal copy require a cited external source; all credits require the existing role-specific verification standard.
3. **Price-positioning tension is managed, not hidden.** Keep published rates (a genuine differentiator and GEO asset for cost queries) but never lead a pillar with price; the prestige pages sell voice first.
4. **Cannibalisation control.** One canonical URL per keyword family; pillars cross-link rather than compete; journal articles target informational terms, pillars transactional terms. The CSV assigns exactly one canonical target per query.
5. **Build integrity.** The repo's verifiers encode hard-won rules (real paths, twin parity, sitemap coverage, 300-word minimum, no orphan items, CSP allowlists, pricing-token sync). Any implementation passes `npm run build` and `npm test` before deploy; generated outputs are never hand-edited.
6. **Privacy stance is load-bearing.** No GA4, no cookies, no beacon allowlisting. Measurement runs through GSC, server logs and manual grids. The Cloudflare-injected beacon stays CSP-blocked.
7. **llms.txt discipline.** Keep the curated manifest small and accurate; Google ignores it and other engines treat it as infrastructure. A stale manifest is worse than none.
8. **"Elevated horror" nuance.** Use the term because buyers search it, but always contextualise the filmmaker controversy; uncritical adoption signals outsider status to the exact audience targeted.
9. **Review markup.** Remove or repair unattributable self-published review schema before pursuing rich results.
10. **Expectation setting.** Ranking is a 3–6 month compounding programme even on thin SERPs; the press release and entity work create the off-site corroboration that on-page work alone cannot.
