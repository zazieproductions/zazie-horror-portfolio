I What folk horror asks of a score

Folk horror rarely frightens with volume. It frightens with belonging: a landscape that predates the characters, a community whose customs keep perfect time, a ritual that continues whether the camera watches or not. The score therefore has a different job from a conventional horror underscore. It must sound grown rather than composed — older than the plot, indifferent to the protagonist, and calm enough that the audience leans in before they understand why.

That is why library music works against this subgenre. Stock “dark folk” tracks are built to be licensed by anyone, which means they belong to no place in particular; folk horror collapses the moment its valley, moor, hollow or harvest field sounds generic. A bespoke folk horror score is spotted against your edit like a location recording: motifs behave like customs, drones behave like weather, and silence is allowed to do the work a jump scare would cheapen.

Directors usually arrive with a temp track they cannot license — a reference score full of bowed, breathy, ritual dread — and an edit that has learned to breathe at its tempo. The commission then is not to imitate that temp track, which would be legally and creatively unsafe, but to build an original instrumentarium for your landscape that produces the same physical effect in the room: unease you feel in the sternum before you can name it.

II The acoustic footprint: bespoke instrument builds, bowed metal, struck strings & hybrid orchestration

The folk horror palette at Zazie Productions is physical first. Sounds are performed, scraped, bowed and recorded before they are processed, so every cue carries the small irregularities — bow hair catching, metal warming into tune, a string struck slightly off-centre — that tell an audience’s nervous system the sound was made by hands, in a room, for this film.

Bespoke instrument builds & bowed metal

Custom and prepared sources give a production a sound no library can resell to the film screening after yours. Bowed metal supplies the long, unstable harmonics folk horror needs for landscape dread: tones that seem to arrive from the field itself rather than from an orchestra pit. Tuned against your picture, they can sit under dialogue as sub-audible pressure or rise into the keening glissandi that turn a procession sinister.

Struck & prepared strings, field recordings

Struck strings, col legno attack and prepared-string resonance provide the ritual pulse — dry, wooden, closer to labour and ceremony than to a drum kit. Layered with musique concrète field material, they let a score braid music and location sound together until the audience cannot tell where the village ends and the composition begins. That blur is the subgenre’s oldest trick, and it only works when the recordings are made for the film.

Hybrid orchestration, kept on a short leash

Hybrid orchestration and custom synthesis extend the acoustic sources — sub-frequency weight under a harvest sequence, spectral processing that stretches a single bowed tone across a whole scene — without sanding off their grain. The aim is dissonant acoustic textures with a human fingerprint, not a polished sample-library orchestra wearing a folk costume.

VI Questions directors ask a folk horror composer

01 What is folk horror custom instrumentation — and why does it matter?

It means the instruments are built, prepared or performed specifically for your production — bowed metal, struck and prepared strings, performed waterphone, field recordings — rather than loaded from a shared sample library. Folk horror depends on a sense of place; a bespoke instrumentarium gives your landscape a sonic identity no other film on the festival programme can share.

02 My edit is temp-tracked with a famous folk horror score. Can you replace it?

Yes — that is the most common folk horror brief. The temp track is treated as a map of function (where dread rises, where ritual breathes, where silence falls), never as material to imitate. You receive an original score that performs the same dramatic work, with clean human authorship for festival, distribution and E&O chain of title.
