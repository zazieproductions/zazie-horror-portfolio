I What dark sci-fi asks of a score

Dark science fiction is horror with the lights of reason still on. Its dread is scale: systems too large to argue with, signals that keep transmitting after the crew stops answering, technology continuing its instructions in an empty room. Music for it must therefore do something orchestral adventure scoring refuses to do — withhold the human reassurance of melody and let timbre, drone and spatial placement carry the argument.

That is why this subgenre belongs to composers who also work as sound artists. The score, the ship tone, the machine voice and the silence between transmissions are one material. Directors and curators searching for an avant-garde festival film composer are usually protecting a premiere — a festival slot, a gallery loop, an experimental programme — where a generic synth pad would expose the work instantly. What they need is a composed sound-world with the rigour of electroacoustic music and the picture discipline of film scoring.

II Drone, signal & spatial dread: the acoustic footprint

Dark atmospheric cinematic drone music

Long-form drone is composed here, not looped: sub-harmonic rumination that evolves over minutes, sensory-deprivation beds that make a room feel pressurised, harmonic fields tuned to your picture’s machinery and architecture. Drone cues are spotted like lighting — they establish the physics of a world and then betray it by a degree at a time.

Cold analog synthesis & electroacoustic processing

Analog walls, tape decay and granular processing give the machine world its voice, while the studio’s acoustic core — bespoke instrument builds, bowed metal, struck strings and hybrid orchestration — keeps a human fingerprint inside it. That friction between performed material and cold synthesis is where dark sci-fi lives: the body, still audible inside the system.

Multichannel spatial audio composition

Work by Zazie Kanwar-Torge has been performed in 20.4-channel spatial audio at Ars Electronica 2026. For film, that practice translates into scores conceived spatially from the first sketch — signals that orbit, drones with architecture, cues designed to fold down honestly to stereo for streaming and open out again for cinema, gallery and installation playback.

On composing for rooms that play work all day

Gallery and festival-loop work makes specific demands — seamless repetition without audible seams, low-frequency behaviour that respects neighbouring works, mixes that survive translation from a tuned cinema to a concrete hall. Those demands are native to a practice built on musique concrète, acousmatic composition and multichannel diffusion, and they are scoped explicitly: stereo WAV is standard delivery, with stems, alternate mixes and surround or immersive versions available as separately quoted specialist work. Atmospheric game worlds draw on the same spatial craft through the adaptive game scoring packages.

VI Questions directors & curators ask a dark sci-fi composer

01 What does an avant-garde sci-fi film scorer do differently?

The score is treated as composed sound art under picture discipline: electroacoustic and musique concrète techniques, bespoke instrument builds and spatial writing are spotted to the frame like conventional cues, but the material itself — drones, signals, processed acoustic sources — is built for the production instead of drawn from orchestral or synth presets.

02 Can you deliver multichannel or spatial audio for festival and gallery playback?

Standard delivery is professional stereo WAV. Multichannel, surround and immersive versions — and gallery-loop preparation — are available as separately scoped specialist work after a feasibility check, drawing on a practice that includes 20.4-channel spatial audio performance at Ars Electronica 2026.
