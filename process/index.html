Psychological horror scoring process: from spotting to delivery

I Inquiry and rough cut review

Start with a short email to zaziediya@gmail.com about the project and the scenes that need music. Format, scored minutes, a target date and tone references help when known; budget is optional, and a rough cut can follow later.

From quote to booking what happens after the first email

A written, nonbinding quote follows review of the relevant picture or scene materials. Nothing is booked until terms are agreed and the deposit is received. See the how-to-start-a-commission FAQ and scoring services.

II Spotting session

The cut plays head to tail with the director and every cue is marked: where music starts, where it stops, what it does dramatically, and where it must stay out of dialogue or sound design. The output is spotting notes, a cue list with timings, tone agreement per cue, and a delivery schedule. Remote by default over video call with the cut playing in sync, see the spotting FAQ.

Spotting is the cheapest place to change your mind: it costs nothing there, and a lot later.

Why the session comes first
  • 01

    Psychological horror intent

    What is the scene actually doing: dread, dissociation, reveal, pursuit, grief, contamination? Each cue gets its dramatic function before a note is written.

  • 02

    Timing and density

    Where the music must stay out of dialogue and sound design. Cue count and scored music minutes drive most of the quote, see what moves the price.

  • 03

    Tone agreement per cue

    Reference tracks and films for tone, plus the showreel cues you temped with and why. Temp love is a normal hazard: say whether you want the score to sound like the temp, or like itself.

III Composition - psychological architecture and dark atmospheric color

Original scores for psychological horror, folk horror, body horror, supernatural thrillers, cosmic horror, techno-thrillers and dark science fiction. Every commissioned cue is written for your picture: no stock, no generative model.

The palette struck strings, bowed metal, hybrid orchestration, custom synths

The palette is struck strings, bowed metal, hybrid orchestration and custom synthesiser builds, written to picture rather than pulled from a library. Hear it in the 30 original horror cues and the selected horror productions.

  • 01

    Psychological architecture

    Slow-burn beds, dissociative harmony, and tension shaped to the cut. Built for psychological horror, folk horror and supernatural thrillers: themes shaped around the story and the cues agreed for the project.

  • 02

    Dark atmospheric color

    Hybrid orchestration, sub-frequency weight, frozen ambient space. Bowed metal, waterphone, custom synths. Atmospheric horror beds and dread sound design.

  • 03

    Cinematic themes and motifs

    Motifs designed to carry the emotional weight of the cut: quiet dread, clear themes, nothing generic, in the words of a collaborator review.

IV Revisions: consolidated rounds, agreed in the quote

The written quote states how many consolidated revision rounds your commission includes, before composition begins. A round is one consolidated set of reasonable changes to an agreed creative direction, send the notes together rather than one at a time.

Extra rounds and recues how changes after agreement are billed

Each extra round beyond the agreed allowance is quoted in writing before work on it begins. New cues, substantial rewrites and recues caused by picture changes after lock are scoped and quoted separately before work proceeds. Confirm the applicable allowance in the written quote. See the revisions FAQ and the cancellation terms.

V Delivery - what lands in the inbox

Standard delivery
ItemDetail
Original score cuesProfessional stereo WAV files (24-bit, 48 kHz unless the project specifies otherwise)
Cue list and timingsAgreed cue titles and timings listed for your edit

Separate deliverables & picture changes

Stems, alternate mixes, surround or immersive formats, final film mixing, dialogue editing and other specialist work are not standard inclusions; they are discussed after a feasibility check and quoted separately. Small trims inside an existing cue are usually absorbed as a courtesy; anything structural: a scene reordered, a sequence dropped, a runtime extended, is a recue, quoted before it is done (see the deliverables FAQ and picture-lock FAQ).

VI Rights, credit, and cue sheet filing

The commercial pricing model describes a standard project-use licence for the named production, its festival run and its own release, with copyright staying with the composer. Expanded distribution, exclusivity, copyright assignment and soundtrack exploitation require a separate written agreement. The operative terms and licensing pages still describe a buyout as the default; those documents have not been changed here, and the applicable rights must be confirmed in the written quote and signed agreement while the documents are reconciled.

Negotiable terms, credit wording and cue sheets what can be changed, and how filing works

Expanded distribution, exclusivity, copyright assignment, soundtrack exploitation and any different publishing split must be scoped in writing; the signed agreement controls. See licensing, rights and credits and the who-owns-the-score FAQ.

On machine training

The standard project-use model does not include machine-learning training rights in the music, stems or session data; the applicable restriction must be confirmed in the written agreement. No commissioned cue is composed by a generative model. See the AI-training FAQ.
