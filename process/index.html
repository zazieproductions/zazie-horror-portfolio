IInquiry and rough cut review

Start with a short email to zaziediya@gmail.com about the project and the scenes that need music. Format, scored minutes, a target date and tone references help when known; budget is optional, and a rough cut can follow later.

From quote to booking what happens after the first email

A written, nonbinding quote follows review of the relevant picture or scene materials. Nothing is booked until terms are agreed and the deposit is received. See the how-to-start-a-commission FAQ and scoring rates.

IISpotting session

The cut plays head to tail with the director and every cue is marked: where music starts, where it stops, what it does dramatically, and where it must stay out of dialogue or sound design. The output is a cue sheet with timings, tone agreement per cue, and a delivery schedule. Remote by default over video call with the cut playing in sync — see the spotting FAQ.

Spotting is the cheapest place to change your mind: it costs nothing there, and a lot later.

Why the session comes first
  • 01

    Psychological horror intent

    What is the scene actually doing — dread, dissociation, reveal, pursuit, grief, contamination? Each cue gets its dramatic function before a note is written.

  • 02

    Timing and density

    Where the music must stay out of dialogue and sound design. Cue count and scored runtime drive most of the quote — see what moves the price.

  • 03

    Tone agreement per cue

    Reference tracks and films for tone, plus the showreel cues you temped with and why. Temp love is a normal hazard: say whether you want the score to sound like the temp, or like itself.

IIIComposition - psychological architecture and dark atmospheric color

Original scores for psychological horror, folk horror, body horror, supernatural thrillers, cosmic horror, techno-thrillers and dark science fiction. Every commissioned cue is written for your picture: no stock, no generative model.

The palette struck strings, bowed metal, hybrid orchestration, custom synths

The palette is struck strings, bowed metal, hybrid orchestration and custom synthesiser builds, written to picture rather than pulled from a library. Hear it in the 32 original horror cues and the selected horror productions.

  • 01

    Psychological architecture

    Slow-burn beds, dissociative harmony, and tension shaped to the cut. Built for psychological horror, folk horror and supernatural thrillers — themes that carry through the film, the trailer and the festival cut.

  • 02

    Dark atmospheric color

    Hybrid orchestration, sub-frequency weight, frozen ambient space. Bowed metal, waterphone, custom synths. Atmospheric horror beds and dread sound design.

  • 03

    Cinematic themes and motifs

    Motifs designed to carry the emotional weight of the cut: quiet dread, clear themes, nothing generic — in the words of a collaborator review.

IVRevisions - two rounds per cue plus consolidation

Two revision rounds per cue during composition, plus one consolidation pass after the full score is delivered. A round is one collected set of notes — send everything at once rather than a note a day.

Extra rounds and recues how changes after agreement are billed

Rounds beyond the included two, and any recue caused by a picture change after lock, are billed as a change order at the rate in the quote — nobody is surprised, because it is agreed at the start. See the revisions FAQ and the cancellation terms.

Separate deliverables & picture changes

Immersive or surround formats, the album master and the soundtrack release master are separate deliverables — see the deliverables FAQ. Small trims inside an existing cue are usually absorbed as a courtesy; anything structural — a scene reordered, a sequence dropped, a runtime extended — is a recue, quoted before it is done (see the picture-lock FAQ).

VIRights, credit, and cue sheet filing

The default grant is a buyout: on payment in full, copyright in the commissioned score passes to the production — for the picture and its versions, in all media and territories, in perpetuity. The composer keeps the writer's share of public performance royalties and PRO affiliation, the right to be credited, and the right to use excerpts in reel, portfolio and press.

Negotiable terms, credit wording and cue sheets what can be changed, and how filing works

An exclusive licence, a non-exclusive licence or a different publishing split can be agreed before the quote; a signed agreement always beats this page. See licensing, rights and credits and the who-owns-the-score FAQ.

End crawl: Music by Zazie Kanwar-Torge. Press and festival listings: Original score by Zazie Kanwar-Torge (Zazie Productions). Copyable wording lives in the licensing page credit section. The production files the cue sheet with the PROs, because it travels with the picture-delivery paperwork; the composer supplies the music side — see the cue-sheet FAQ.

On machine training

The default grant does not licence the music, stems or session data for machine-learning training, and no commissioned cue is composed by a generative model. See the AI-training FAQ.
