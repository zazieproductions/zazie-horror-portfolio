I How much does it cost to score a 10-minute short film?

It depends on how much of those ten minutes actually carries music — and that is good news. Composers who price by runtime charge you for dialogue, silence and credits. Scored-minute pricing charges only for original music composed to picture. Count the moments where music must do dramatic work — openings, turns, reveals, climaxes, buttons — and most 10-minute shorts land in one of three honest scenarios:

Your 10-minute short needs…PackagePublished starting feeTypical published band
One pivotal sequence scored; the rest plays dry or uses licensed material (1–2 scored minutes) Individual Scene Scoring From $250 USD $250–$550
A complete short score: opening world, rising dread, climax and button (3–5 scored minutes) Focused Short-Film Score From $650 USD $650–$1,350
Near wall-to-wall music or a developed thematic score (6–12 scored minutes) Extended Short-Film Score From $1,500 USD $1,500–$2,650

So for the most common case — a festival short whose score carries the story in three to five composed minutes — indie short film composer rates here begin at From $650 USD, inside a published band of $650–$1,350 before complexity, schedule and licence adjustments. Each package publishes a starting fee, not a guaranteed maximum amount of music. A complex two-minute composition may require a higher quote than a simple atmospheric cue of the same length. The estimator below runs the same arithmetic the written quote starts from, in public, with no email required.

Pricing follows original music being commissioned, not total film runtime. A 90-minute feature may need 2 scored minutes; a 6-minute short may need 9. A single scene or cue for a feature may use Individual Scene Scoring. The $2,500 indicative minimum applies only to substantial feature-film or experimental-cinema scoring engagements.

III What you actually get at each tier

A starting fee is only useful if you know what stands behind it. In director’s terms:

Individual Scene Scoring — From $250 USD

A single sequence or moment composed to picture: a dread bed, a tension cue, a reveal or a stinger-led transition. Open to shorts and features alike when only one cue is needed. You get one sequence composed and performed to your locked (or near-locked) picture, one consolidated revision round to tune it against the edit, and mix-ready stereo WAV delivery with cue timings — built for festival scenes, pitch sequences and the single pivotal moment in a longer edit, features included.

Focused Short-Film Score — From $650 USD

A complete short film or a fully scored act, with its own motifs, a spotting session and a consistent sonic world. Up to 5 scored minutes. You get a spotting session that maps every cue against the cut, an original motif family and sonic world designed for your film’s subgenre, the full cue set composed to picture, one consolidated revision round, and the same professional delivery — built for narrative and experimental shorts where the score carries the story from the first frame.

Extended Short-Film Score — From $1,500 USD

Longer shorts, multi-part short work or a developed theme set with a larger cue list and more tonal range. Up to 12 scored minutes. You get everything in the focused tier at larger scale — a developed theme set across a bigger cue list, two consolidated revision rounds, and delivery built for edits where music is a structural character — longer shorts, series pilots and projects that need a developed thematic score.

IV What moves the quote (and what never does)

Five factors move a written quote inside — and occasionally above — the published bands. All five are visible in the estimator before you ever send an email:

  • Scored minutes & cue count. Two cues per scored minute are included; each additional cue is $50 before other adjustments. Many short cues cost more than few long ones, because each is a separate composition.
  • Musical complexity. A focused palette is the base rate. Layered hybrid writing and bespoke or experimental work — custom instrument builds, performed waterphone and bowed metal, unconventional structures — carry published multipliers, because they are literally more studio days.
  • Schedule. Standard schedules carry no surcharge. Expedited and rush festival deadlines carry published uplifts and are always confirmed in writing, subject to availability.
  • Revisions. A revision round means one consolidated set of reasonable changes to an agreed creative direction. New cues, substantial rewrites, and changes caused by picture recuts are separately quoted. Extra consolidated rounds beyond your package allowance are $75 minimum or 10% of the fee each, whichever is greater.
  • Licence. Composition is priced separately from rights. The standard project-use licence is included; expanded distribution and exclusivity adjust the fee under a separate written agreement, and a copyright buyout or soundtrack album is always quoted individually.

What never moves the price: your film’s total runtime, its festival ambitions, or how the negotiation feels. Payment is equally plain: 50% deposit before work begins. Remaining 50% due before release of final approved deliverables, subject to the written agreement. Applies to new commissions only; existing contracts are unchanged. The full factor table lives on the rate card, and the commissioning mechanics on the process page.

VI Indie short film composer rates: direct answers

01 How much does it cost to score a 10 minute short film?

At Zazie Productions, a typical 10-minute short needing 3–5 minutes of original music commissions the Focused Short-Film Score: From $650 USD, within a published band of $650–$1,350 before complexity, schedule and licence adjustments. A single scored sequence starts at $250; near wall-to-wall scores start at $1,500. Price follows scored music, never the 10-minute runtime itself.

02 What are indie short film composer rates per minute of music?

This studio publishes bands rather than a flat per-minute rate, because a complex two-minute cue can cost more than a simple five-minute bed. The bands are: 1–2 scored minutes From $250 (Individual Scene Scoring), 3–5 minutes From $650 (Focused Short-Film Score), and 6–12 minutes From $1,500 (Extended Short-Film Score), all USD starting fees. The estimator interpolates your exact minute count inside those bands.

03 How do I count my film’s scored minutes?

Play the cut and note every stretch where music must carry emotion, transition or dread — openings, montages, reveals, climaxes, credits buttons. Add those durations together. Dialogue scenes that play dry do not count. If you are unsure, send the scene list anyway: counting scored minutes together is exactly what the spotting session is for.

04 What is included in the Focused Short-Film Score starting fee?

Original music composed to picture for the agreed cue list (Approximately 3–5 minutes of original music), a spotting session, one consolidated revision round, professional stereo WAV delivery at 24-bit/48 kHz, and a cue list with timings for your edit. Stems, alternate mixes, surround versions, live players and soundtrack mastering are separately scoped specialist work, listed in full in section III above.

05 My project is a feature. Do short-film rates still apply to one scene?

Yes. A feature commissioning one scene or cue uses Individual Scene Scoring from $250 USD; the indicative $2,500 minimum applies only to substantial full feature-film or experimental-cinema scoring engagements, which are custom quoted after review of the cut.

06 Why not just license stock music for a fraction of the price?

Sometimes you should — the hiring guide says so plainly, and the studio catalogue licenses ready-made material. Commission when the cue must hit your exact frames, carry your film’s motif, or give a festival programme a sound no other film shares. The honest comparison is not price per track; it is price per minute of music that could only belong to your film.
