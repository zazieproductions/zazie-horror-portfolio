Subgenre focus · internal tension

Psychological horror scoring for the feeling beneath the frame.

Psychological horror rarely needs a constant wall of sound. It needs a score that understands denial, repetition, dissociation, anticipation, and the instant a familiar space becomes wrong.

Hear a psychological cueSee work on picture

Compositional language

Make uncertainty audible.

The portfolio’s cue list includes “Needle in The Nerve,” “Dissociative Amnesia,” “The Room Forgets You,” and “Subdued Drone / Sensory Deprivation.” Their titles describe the territory: internal fracture, memory pressure, held breath, and sound that refuses to resolve too soon.

01 · Restraint

Silence as pressure

Leave room for performance and room tone. A restrained bed can make the audience listen for the sound that has not arrived yet.

02 · Motif

Recognition, then corruption

A small interval, timbre, or pulse can return with one element altered. The scene stays coherent while the character’s certainty does not.

03 · Texture

Unstable surfaces

Bow, scrape, distort, filter, and layer acoustic or synthetic material when the texture needs to feel tactile rather than merely “scary.”

Useful for

Slow-burn dread, thriller escalation, and altered states.

Psychological thriller scoring and psychological horror scoring overlap in their attention to subjectivity. The brief may call for a pulse under a conversation, a motif that outlives a character, or an ending that denies clean resolution. The spotting decision comes before the adjective.

For directors, the useful starting material is a cut, a scene map, or a clear sequence of references—not a request to imitate a named film.
