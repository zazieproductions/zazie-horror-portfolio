01 · Biography

Bio

Zazie Kanwar-Torge is an award-winning film composer, experimental producer, and multi-instrumentalist specializing in experimental psychological horror for independent and arthouse cinema, folk horror, body horror, supernatural thrillers, and dark science fiction. Through Zazie Productions, he creates original film scores and immersive sonic atmospheres that explore dread, dissonance, isolation, and the uncanny.

Working at the intersection of cinematic scoring, musique concrète, and electroacoustic composition, his distinctive palette combines bowed metal, struck strings, experimental synthesis, and hybrid orchestration. His work has been featured at Ars Electronica 2026, performed in 20.4-channel spatial audio, and selected for international experimental music and film programs, including MUSLAB/Cero Records and EXiS in Seoul. Previously commissioned by the Black Mountain College Museum + Arts Center, he also develops original audio instruments and experimental sound libraries.

Drawing on a background in experimental film, sound art, and audiovisual composition, Zazie approaches each score as an extension of the film's psychological and emotional world, creating visceral, atmospheric music tailored to independent shorts, feature films, series, and horror video games.

FAQ · Before you commit

What filmmakers ask before the first cue

Timing, budget, fit, availability and pricing, answered plainly and in the open. The full detail on process, rights and delivery lives in the complete FAQ.

01We are still in pre-production or mid-shoot. Is it too early to bring you on?

Actually, the earlier, the better. The greatest horror films (e.g., The Shining, Hereditary, Under the Skin) were scored alongside filming. When you bring me on early, we can stay coordinated from the beginning. It can help establish a sonic identity before temporary music starts steering creative decisions. You don’t need a locked cut, just a clear sense of what you’re trying to make.

02I’m a low-budget filmmaker. Can you still help me?

Tell me the real number. A focused musical palette, a smaller cue count, or a carefully chosen amount of score can sometimes serve a film better than wall-to-wall music. If the scope and budget don’t align, you’ll know before committing.

03How do I know you’ll actually be available for my timeline?

I take on no more than four feature engagements per year. As of this writing, three slots remain for Q4. Every email is answered personally within 72 hours, even when the answer is no.

04What kinds of projects are you the best fit for?

Psychological horror, folk horror, body horror, slow-burn thrillers, experimental cinema, atmospheric games, A24-style art house, and projects that live between categories. If mood, texture, and emotional specificity matter to you, we’re already speaking the same language.

05I like your work, but I already have a composer. Any options?

Yes. If you need additional support, whether for a single sequence, a key nightmare scene, or one dissonant section, don’t hesitate to reach out.

06How do you calculate your pricing or rates for a film score?

Every score is quoted individually because no two productions share the same creative scale. There is no rigid, published retail rate card. Your written quotation is custom-built around your specific format, the exact minutes of music required, your timeline deadlines, and your stated budget allocation. We work transparently to align the musical scope with your project’s financial reality.

07Can you collaborate remotely with production teams in different time zones?

Yes! My post-production workflow is optimized for remote, borderless collaboration. I routinely score independent projects, theatrical shorts, and interactive media properties for directors, showrunners, and producers across North America, Europe, and international festival circuits with zero logistical friction.
