I. Starting a project

01 What kinds of projects are a fit?

The studio focuses on original music for dark, experimental screen work, including independent film, series, trailers and selected games. Browse the project archive or listen to the showreel to hear the work.

02 What should I include in an inquiry?

A short description, format, approximate scored minutes, target date and any useful references are enough to begin. A rough cut is welcome when available. The inquiry form opens a message in your email application.

03 What does the written quote cover?

The quote describes the agreed scope, fee, schedule, revision allowance, deliverables, payment arrangements and applicable rights. The quote and any project agreement should be reviewed together before acceptance; the terms explain document precedence.

II. Scope and delivery

04 How is the schedule set?

Timing is planned around the picture, scored minutes, cue density and delivery date. The schedule is confirmed with the written scope; changes to the cut or requested work may require a revised schedule. See the scoring workflow.

05 How do revisions work?

The written quote states the included revision allowance and how feedback is collected. New cues, picture changes or requests beyond the agreed scope are discussed and quoted before additional work begins.

06 Which files are delivered?

Delivery formats are named in the quote. Stereo WAVs are common; stems, alternate mixes, surround formats, live players and separate sound-design work are scoped individually.

III. Interactive work

07 Can you score a game?

Selected game projects are considered. Interactive music needs are scoped around the game’s implementation, states and milestones rather than a locked picture; middleware, integration and implementation responsibilities must be agreed separately.
