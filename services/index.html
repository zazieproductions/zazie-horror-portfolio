I What you can commission

Original music composed to your picture: quoted per project, with the scope built around the budget you state and the references you share. There are no packages and no published fees to fit your film into; describe the music you need and the quote follows the work.

  • A single scene or cue: a dread bed, a tension cue, a reveal or a stinger-led transition, in a short or inside a feature.
  • A complete short-film score: a fully scored short or act, with its own motifs, a spotting session and one consistent sonic world.
  • A feature or experimental-cinema score: longer-form and multi-part work, installation and expanded scopes, with live players or complex delivery where the project calls for them.
  • Series and episodic work: episode scores and recurring thematic material across a run.
  • Trailers and teasers: short-form cues built for the cut, or adaptation of an existing score.
  • Games and interactive work: adaptive states, seamless loops and stingers; see game scoring.
  • Sound-design-led work: designed dread, atmospheres and transitions; see sound design.

A quote follows the music being commissioned, not the length of your film.

The quoting principle · Zazie Productions

There is no published rate card: every commission is quoted individually against the budget, scope and references you share. The written quote (issued after review of your cut, cue list or scene notes) states the scope, revision rounds, licence and fee for your project, and it is the only binding figure. Quotes are nonbinding until accepted in writing; taxes, live players and third-party clearances are additional where they apply.

II Included in every commission

A commission is a composition service: original music written to your picture for the cues agreed in writing. Licence scope, copyright and soundtrack rights are separate and are described in section III. Anything not on this list is quoted on request.

What’s includedStandard delivery & optional add-ons

Included in every commission

  • Original music synchronized to picture for the cues agreed in writing.
  • Agreed musical scope and cue deliverables confirmed in writing before composition begins: spotting notes, cue list and tone references.
  • Consolidated revision rounds, with the number of included rounds stated in the written quote.
  • Professional stereo WAV delivery (24-bit, 48 kHz unless your project specifies otherwise).
  • Cue titles and timings listed for your edit.

Specialist work, quoted on request

Discussed individually after a feasibility check. Not standard inclusions; quoted per project on request.

  • Alternate, tension-variant or clean mixes
  • Surround or immersive mastering (5.1, 7.1, Dolby Atmos)
  • Final film mix, dialogue editing or dub-stage supervision
  • Comprehensive audio post-production
  • Bespoke sound-design packages, foley and effects
  • Live musicians, soloists and session costs
  • Soundtrack album mastering and distribution
  • Adaptive or interactive music implementation for game engines
  • Trailer or teaser cut adaptation

III Licence options

Every commission is licensed for the named production under a standard project-use licence (its festival run and its own release), with copyright staying with the composer. Expanded distribution, exclusivity, copyright assignment and soundtrack exploitation require a separate written agreement. The operative licensing and terms pages still describe a buyout as the default, so applicable rights must be confirmed in the written quote and signed agreement while those documents are reconciled.

  • Standard project-use licenceUse of the recorded score in the named production, its festival run and its own release, as set out in the written agreement. Copyright stays with the composer.
  • Expanded distributionAdds broadcast, streaming and paid video-on-demand to the project licence, by separate written agreement.
  • Exclusive licenceExclusive to the production for an agreed term and territory, by separate written agreement. The composer keeps the copyright.
  • Copyright assignment (buyout)Transfer of copyright on payment. Always under a separate written agreement; never included automatically.
  • Soundtrack album releaseCommercial release of the score as an album. Separate agreement, discussed per project.

IV How your quote is shaped

Nothing here is priced from a table: these are the things a written quote weighs, in the order it weighs them. Your stated budget comes first, the scope is built around it honestly, or a smaller scope or licensing existing material is suggested instead.

Your budget
Tell me the range you have set aside for music and the scope is built around it honestly: a focused cue set, a fuller score, or licensing existing material where that serves the film better. “Not sure yet” is a workable answer; the quote still itemizes exactly what it covers.
Scored music
Minutes of finished music, not film runtime. A fourteen-minute short with three minutes of score is quoted on three minutes; nothing scales with how long the film is.
Cues
How many separate pieces the score needs. The cue list is agreed at spotting and named in the quote; a cue added after the list is locked is quoted before it is written.
Musical complexity
A focused palette (a few sources, one motif family), a layered hybrid (orchestral and electronic textures, several themes), or bespoke and experimental work (custom builds, performed waterphone, bowed metal, unconventional structure). A complex two-minute composition can quote higher than a simple atmospheric cue of the same length.
Schedule
A comfortable schedule is the easiest to quote. Tight turnarounds are subject to availability and are confirmed in writing before a quote is accepted.
Revisions
A revision round means one consolidated set of reasonable changes to an agreed creative direction. New cues, substantial rewrites, and changes caused by picture recuts are separately quoted. The number of included rounds is stated in the written quote; additional rounds are quoted before work on them begins.
Licence
Composition and rights are separate lines in the same agreement. The standard project-use licence covers the named production; wider distribution, exclusivity, a copyright buyout or a soundtrack album each need a separate written agreement.
Live players & specialist delivery
Session musicians, stems, surround or immersive masters, album mastering and game-engine implementation are discussed per project after a feasibility check, never assumed.
Payment
50% deposit before work begins. Remaining 50% due before release of final approved deliverables, subject to the written agreement. Applies to new commissions only; existing contracts are unchanged.

The binding figure

There are no published fees to estimate from. The written quote, issued after review of your materials, is the only figure, and the only binding one.

V What a written quote actually contains

A figure on its own is not a scope. Every quote issued for a commission states the same five things, in the same order, so two quotes can be compared line by line rather than by the number at the bottom.

Composition scope
The scored minutes and the cue count, stated in whole minutes and whole cues. Minutes mean finished music, not film runtime: a fourteen-minute short with three minutes of score is quoted on three minutes. Nothing in a quote scales with how long the film is.
Cue list
Each cue named by working title with the in-point and out-point agreed at spotting. The cue count is fixed in the quote; a cue added after the list is locked is quoted before it is written, never absorbed silently. The scoring process covers how cues are spotted and named in the first place.
Revision allowance
The number of consolidated rounds included, stated plainly in the quote. A round is one consolidated set of reasonable changes to an agreed creative direction; new cues, substantial rewrites and changes caused by a recut are quoted separately.
Licence
The named production, its festival run and its own release under the standard project-use licence, or one of the wider options in section III. Composition and licence are stated as separate lines, and both appear in the same agreement: licensing, rights and credits sets out what each option grants and what the composer keeps.
Deliverables
The exact files and the date: stereo WAV at 24-bit / 48 kHz unless the project specifies otherwise, plus the cue list with titles and timings for your edit. Stems by family, alternates, surround masters, soundtrack mastering and game-engine implementation are specialist items, quoted on request rather than assumed.

What a quote never assumes

These are excluded by default because they are either supplied by the production or bought in per project. Any of them can be added to a quote on request; they are listed here so that a budget review does not discover them three weeks before delivery.

  • Live players, session fees, studio hire, instrument rental and travel to a scoring stage.
  • Third-party sample or library clearances, re-recording fees and publishing or sync administration on outside material.
  • Final film mix, dialogue editing, foley, dub-stage supervision and deliverables beyond the score itself.
  • Trailer, teaser or vertical-cut adaptation of a delivered cue.
  • Recomposition after a recut below picture lock, or a change of creative direction mid-score.
  • Taxes, withholding and platform or banking fees.

Delivery, handover and what happens to the files

Music is delivered as finished stereo mixes, named by cue number and title, with a cue list that maps every file to its in-point and out-point. If your editor works in a different frame rate or needs a guide mix at a lower sample rate, say so when the scope is agreed rather than after delivery. Where the picture changes after delivery within the agreed revision allowance, the change is handled as a revision; where the change is a new edit of the scene, it is a new cue and is quoted as one.

Nothing you send is published or passed to a third party. Rough cuts, scripts and reference tracks stay confidential, and a mutual non-disclosure agreement is available before anything is shared. The scoring inquiry page explains what happens to your message; the privacy notice covers the site itself, and the commissioning terms cover deposits, cancellation and acceptance.

Before you accept a quote

A full buyout (transfer of copyright) and a soundtrack album are quoted individually once the rights you need are discussed, never bundled silently into a composition quote. Licence terms are set out in the written quote; the studio's standard licensing terms are being reviewed, so read them before you accept.

Compare like with like

When comparing quotes from different composers, compare what is included: scored minutes, cue count, revision rounds, stems, licence scope and delivery format. A lower figure with one revision round and no distribution rights is not the same offer as a higher one that includes both. The hiring guide sets out the questions worth asking before either quote is signed.

VI How a commission starts

  1. Inquiry. Send a scoring inquiry with your format, the music you need, your budget range and your musical references, rough answers are fine, and a cut link can follow later.
  2. Quote. Receive a written, nonbinding quote that sets out the scope, the revision rounds and the licence.
  3. Agreement. Scope and payment terms are confirmed in writing before composition begins.
  4. Delivery. Spotting, composition, the agreed consolidated revision rounds, then the agreed WAV cues and cue list.
