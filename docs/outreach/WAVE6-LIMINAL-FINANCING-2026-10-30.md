# Wave 6 — the liminal lane where the door is actually open

**Date:** 2026-10-30
**Status:** 1 draft remaining (*The Mire*). *Aurora Comes Home* and *Spiral* were
sent by hand from the account on 30 Oct 2026 at 11:58 and 11:59 EDT — see
"Two went out" below for exactly what those two letters said.

## The finding, stated plainly

The films everyone would name are shut. *Backrooms* is out and Kane Parsons
co-scored it. *Backrooms 2* is his again. *The Space Between* has a composer
(Tyler Leahy). *Junction Row* premiered at Fantasia in July. *Hinter* premiered
at Beyond Fest on 2 October. *The Pools* opens 25 December 2026, so its score is
recorded. *It Ends* is a Neon release. *Dooba Dooba* came out in January on a
micro-budget.

That is the whole top of the subgenre, and every one of them is either closed or
too small to pay properly.

So the realistic version of this ask is not "find a liminal film in post and
grab the scoring job". It is **find the films in the lane that are financed but
not yet shot**, because that is the window where a composer gets attached, and
it is the only window where someone without a *Backrooms*-scale credit is
genuinely in the running. This wave is built on that.

## Why these three and not a longer list

Because every other candidate failed one of four tests, and the failures are
worth recording so the search isn't repeated:

- **Closed** — *Backrooms 2*, *The Pools*, *The Space Between*, *Junction Row*,
  *Hinter*, *It Ends*, *Dooba Dooba*, *The Atlas Field* (DVD out September),
  *Play House* (Fantastic Fest premiere, Divide/Conquer — already drafted in
  wave 3).
- **Too small to be a paid opportunity** — *Final Transmissions* ($10k),
  *Van Life*, *The Willowbrook Tapes*, most of the October indie roundups.
  These are made for less than a composer's fee.
- **No reachable inbox** — Sostis Productions (*Hinter*, Pasadena) publishes a
  contact form only, and *Hinter* has already premiered anyway. Black Samphire
  (Candid Broads / Silicon Gothic) is still the best timing in the lane and
  still has no email, only a form plus an explicit "we do not read unsolicited
  material" notice.
- **Studio-managed** — *Liminal* (Apple, Louis Leterrier), *Mandela Catalogue*
  (Amblin/UA), *V/H/S: SCP* (A24) — covered in wave 5, and music is decided
  above the level a cold email reaches.

## The copy

Because these are pre-shoot, the emails cannot pretend to be scoring a locked
film. They do three things instead: name the specific narrative mechanism that
makes the project interesting, offer one concrete musical method for that
mechanism, and say out loud that the timing is early — then convert that into an
advantage ("a known quantity at the budgeting stage rather than a stranger at
picture lock").

The P.S. changes for this wave. Instead of "one paid cue in the finished film",
it offers **one paid cue for the pitch or the proof-of-concept**. At financing
stage that is the genuinely useful, genuinely paid piece of work: a producer
with a market presentation needs music for a sizzle or a proof-of-concept, and
it is a normal line item. It is also the cheapest possible way for them to find
out whether they want to hire a composer.

| Project | The idea in the email |
|---------|----------------------|
| Aurora Comes Home | One lullaby, two versions: the daughter's clean, the returning woman's a quarter-tone flat with another house's room tone under it. The audience knows she's wrong before the family does |
| Spiral | Score it as an art installation at a fixed tempo too slow for the cutting, which then starts responding to Kat — and gives the score a diegetic reason to exist in a film about the art world |
| The Mire | The bog is the instrument (hydrophone, pitched down and bowed); the priest's chorale is concert pitch and drifts back into the village's folk intonation as his authority fails |

## Honest limits

- **These are not scoring jobs yet.** They are relationships opened at the stage
  where the job gets created. A reply may be "not until next year". That is a
  good outcome and should be treated as one.
- Frontières selection means the project pitched to financiers, not that it is
  fully funded. Both Canadian projects may still be short of their budget; the
  emails say so rather than assuming.
- "No composer attached" is inferred from published project materials. A
  producer may already have someone in mind and simply not have announced it.
- All three addresses are published by the companies themselves, not scraped or
  pattern-guessed. Two are a named producer's own inbox; one is a company
  general address.
- Nothing is sent, scheduled, or tracked. No attachments, no HTML, no pixel.

## Warmth without fiction

There was a request to pre-warm these letters with things that are not quite
true: having seen the film at a named festival, having followed the project on
some site, a friend-of-a-friend who backed the crowdfunding. That is not going
in the copy, for three practical reasons rather than a scruple about white lies.

1. **These are the exact claims that get tested.** "Which screening?" is a
   one-line reply, and a producer who sends it is being friendly, not hostile.
   The friend-of-a-friend version is worse: it invites a follow-up conversation
   that has to be sustained, then has to end.
2. **The downside is asymmetric.** Getting caught inventing one connection
   discounts the true parts of the letter — the *Kryptic* observation, the
   lullaby idea — because the reader now has to audit everything. Canadian genre
   is about a dozen companies who all know each other; the story travels.
3. **It costs nothing to do it honestly.** Everything the fabricated versions
   were reaching for is already achieved by being specific about public
   material, which is what these letters do. Quoting Harjusaari's own line about
   who carries what reads like familiarity, and it happens to be true.

### What replaces it

**Say the cold part out loud.** The *Mire* draft now opens "I'm writing cold, so
I'll be quick and specific." Naming it is disarming: the reader stops waiting
for the connection that isn't there and starts reading the argument. A letter
that pretends to be warm and isn't reads as a mailout; a letter that admits it's
cold reads as a person.

**Be specific where it's checkable.** Precision is the actual signal of having
followed a project. Anyone can claim to have been at a market; almost nobody
writes to you about your own director's quote, or knows the difference between
the Frontières Platform and the Market (Platform is Cannes in May, Market is
Montréal in July, and *Spiral* was in one while the other two were in the other).

**Put the warmth in the follow-up, and earn it first.** The follow-up at 12–14
days is where a real connection is worth more than a claimed one, because by
then it can be true. Before it goes out: watch *Kryptic* (Goodbye) and
*Hatching* (Silva Mysterium). Then the follow-up can honestly say "I watched
*Kryptic* after sending that, and the cue under the tunnel is the reason I
pushed" — which is a far better sentence than any claim about a festival, and
takes one evening to make true.

**If a real connection is wanted, buy it with a day of work.** The Frontières
Music Pitch strand is the actual warm route: four composers, selected, pitching
to the same financiers these producers pitched to. Applying in 2027 turns every
one of these letters into a warm one, legitimately.

### The test to apply before sending

Ask of any line: **does it survive a one-question reply?** If the honest answer
to "oh, which one?" is uncomfortable, cut the line. If the line is about Zazie
rather than about them — "I've had the *Mire* logline open in a tab since July",
"I've been meaning to watch *Hatching* for a year and finally did" — then it is
hers to make true before the send, and it can stay.

Lines that survive: taste, attention, craft opinion, a question about their
film. Lines that don't: attendance, acquaintanceship, funding participation,
anything with a venue or a name attached.
