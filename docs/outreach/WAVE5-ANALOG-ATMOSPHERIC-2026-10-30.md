# Wave 5 — analog and atmospheric, with money attached

**Date:** 2026-10-30
**Status:** 3 drafts written, none sent.

Wave 4 targeted individual films. This wave went after the analog / liminal /
atmospheric lane specifically, following the commercial breakout of *Backrooms*
($10m budget, roughly $394m worldwide) and *Obsession*. That success has pulled
real financing into a subgenre that used to cost nothing to make.

## Why only three

The analog lane is unusually concentrated, and most of the obvious targets fail
a filter. This is a short list because the honest answer is short — not because
the search was short. Each of the three below clears all four tests: a named
project at a real stage, money attached, no composer published on it, and an
inbox the company publishes itself.

| # | Target | The project | Money | Contact | Why now |
|---|--------|-------------|-------|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Paper Street Pictures** (Austin) | *The Mandela Catalogue* — Alex Kister directing, from the 100m+ view analog series | Amblin (Spielberg, Holly Bario) + United Artists (Scott Stuber) + Amazon MGM; Paper Street's Aaron Koontz producing | `info@paperstreetpictures.com` (published on their site; LinkedIn lists the same address for professional inquiries) | Pre-production. The IP is the biggest analog property not yet shot |
| 2 | **Image Nation** (Abu Dhabi / studios arm) | *V/H/S: SCP* — SCP Foundation found-footage anthology, A24 involved, 2027 | Multi-title partnership with Spooky Pictures; also backing *System*, shooting now with Joachim Rønning | `info@imagenation.ae` (published company address) | Two live projects in the lane, one shooting |
| 3 | **Elation Pictures** (London) | slate inquiry rather than one title | *The Damned* (UK/Ireland/Iceland/Belgium co-pro, BFI + Screen Ireland + Wallimage) | `emilie@elationpictures.co.uk` (published in the British Council film database) | A house whose whole identity is atmospheric horror, between projects |

### The strategic point in #2

An anthology is not one score. *V/H/S: SCP* is five or six segments with
different directors, which means five or six small commissions under one roof.
That is the most realistic entry point in this entire campaign for a composer
without a feature credit at that scale — and the email says so plainly. The same
logic applies to *Final Transmissions*, *V/H/S/Mixtape* and every other anthology
in the lane.

## Dropped, and why

Checked, then rejected:

- **Backrooms 2** (A24, Kane Parsons) — Parsons composed roughly half of the
  first film himself. No lane in.
- **Play House** — premiered at Fantastic Fest; executive produced by
  Divide/Conquer, already drafted in wave 3. Score is done.
- **The Space Between** (liminal horror, Midwestern mall) — composer already
  attached (Tyler Leahy), per the film's own credits.
- **I Have Proof** (Bressack, shot on VHS-C) — genuinely analog, but premiered
  21 September 2026 and made at a scale well below the brief.
- **The Peril at Pincer Point** (Fuzz on the Lens, atmospheric B&W, Tony Todd's
  last film) — already premiered at FrightFest.
- **Black Samphire** (Candid Broads / Silicon Gothic, folk horror with Stephen
  Fry and Cathy Tyson) — the best *timing* of anything I found (late development,
  about to crew up) but the company publishes a contact form only and states it
  does not read unsolicited material. No inbox, so no draft. Worth revisiting if
  a producer email surfaces.
- **Dooba Dooba** (Dark Sky) — analog aesthetic, shot, unreleased. Score done.
- **Final Transmissions** — real analog anthology, $10,000 budget. Below brief.
- **Spooky Pictures** directly — the actual producing entity behind *V/H/S: SCP*
  and *System*, but it publishes no inbox; it is a division of Vertigo. Routed
  via Image Nation instead, which is its financing partner and does publish one.
- **Cweature Features** (Waypoint's new genre label) and **Tailored Films**
  (Hokum co-producers) — both already drafted in wave 3; do not duplicate.

## The copy

Same architecture, tuned to analog. The genre's difficulty is that its cheapness
*is* the effect — tape grain, 4:3, floor-level lighting — so a bigger budget can
sand the wrongness off. Each email takes that as its opening problem, which is a
more credible way in than praising the film.

| Target | The idea in the email |
|--------|----------------------|
| Paper Street | Score the absence: the Alternates mimic, so never play the resolving note. Print the score to VHS-C and re-record it, so the damage is physical, not a plugin |
| Image Nation | One inherited archive tone — a low sine at a filing cabinet's resonance plus a fixed hiss bed — mixed under every segment so the anthology feels like one recovered object |
| Elation | Record the landscape before the picture is cut, then build the score only from those recordings, so the instruments are the place |

The Paper Street email also concedes, in the body, that the music on *Mandela*
may be decided above a boutique producer's head — then gives the real reason for
writing: their own slate (*The Artifice Girl*). That concession is deliberate. A
cold email that pretends not to know how the decision gets made reads as
ignorant, and producers notice.

## Known limits

- Paper Street and Image Nation are both **routes to a decision-maker, not the
  decision-maker**. On a Spielberg/Amazon film, a composer is chosen with agency
  involvement; the realistic outcome of these two emails is a relationship and a
  smaller project, not the *Mandela* score. They are written to be honest about
  that and to stay useful either way.
- Image Nation's `info@` is a company-wide address, not a production contact.
  Graded C. Expect it to be screened.
- "No composer attached" is inferred from published credits on each project, not
  confirmed by the productions.
- The Elation email is a slate inquiry, not a film inquiry. It has the weakest
  stage evidence of the three and the strongest address (a named producer's own
  inbox, published by the British Council).
- Nothing is sent, scheduled, or tracked. No attachments, no HTML, no pixel.
