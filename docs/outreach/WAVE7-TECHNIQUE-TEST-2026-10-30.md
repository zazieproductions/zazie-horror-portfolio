## The four engines, written down

**1. Reverse qualification.** Name the conditions under which the reader should
ignore you, first, before any claim. It reads as confidence because a needy
sender never volunteers an exit. Applied: "This is probably not for you, and
I'll say why up front so you can delete it fast."

**2. Scarcity engineering.** A real, self-imposed limit on capacity — two scores
a year, one committed — plus a visible willingness to leave the second slot
empty. This only works if Zazie can say it out loud on a call, so before sending
she should be comfortable with the sentence "two a year is what I can do well".
If that isn't true, change the number rather than the sentiment.

**3. Identity binding around non-neediness.** The letter asserts what she *is*
and explicitly declines to assert need: "I'm not asking for a slot on a slate,
and I'm not going to pretend to need the work." Inverts the usual asymmetry —
the reader is being chosen, not pitched.

**4. Low-risk reciprocity.** Give first, with no invoice and no string attached:
a written read on whether a rough cut's sound is already working, with a
standing promise to say "leave it alone" when that's the answer. For a label or
a sales company that acquires finished films, this is genuinely useful and costs
a paragraph rather than a day of writing music — it is consulting, not spec
scoring, so it stays inside the paid-only rule.

**The adjacency claim, in one line each.** All four letters make the same
argument in different words, and it happens to be true: the people who can write
dissonant, textural, physically-recorded music and the people who can deliver on
a schedule are two different small groups, and the intersection is rarer than
either. That is the only thing in these letters that isn't available to any
other composer, so it carries the weight in all four.

## How to read the results

Four letters, four engines, one variable each. When replies land, the useful
question is not "did they answer" but "which sentence did they answer".

- A reply that engages the **capacity limit** ("what are you working on now?")
  means scarcity was the lever.
- A reply that takes the **free read** means reciprocity was; note that this
  produces a second email, which is worth more than a yes/no.
- A reply that argues with the **reverse qualification** ("actually we do have
  something") means the exit door did the work.
- Silence from all four says the problem is not the letter but the address —
  general inboxes at companies this size are read by someone who is not the
  decision-maker.

No tracking pixels, no HTML, no attachments. Plain text, from the default
address, one follow-up at 12–14 days maximum.
