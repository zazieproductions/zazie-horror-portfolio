**Date:** 2026-10-30
**Scope:** 45 new production companies that make atmospheric, dissonant or

## 1. Why these 45 and not A24 itself

The brief is "A24 type" work, not A24 the company. A24, NEON and the streamers
attach composers through agents and music supervisors, and a cold email to a
$50M film is wasted effort. The realistic version of that aesthetic is the
company one tier down: the producers who make the £2–15M film that *looks* like
an A24 release, hire the composer directly, and are in post six to nine months
after we speak.

Every company below is a **production company with a slate**, not a sales agent
or a distributor — they commission scores rather than package them. Every one
has a first-party or official-industry-listing contact route. **No contact here
is a guess from a data broker's masked pattern.**

## 2. Timing logic (the part that decides whether the email lands)

A composer is normally hired between **the director's cut and picture lock** —
after the film has a shape, before the mix. So the drafts do not ask for a
meeting "sometime"; each one asks about **the window when a cut is locking**,
and each one says the ask is 15 minutes, no deck, no pressure.

| Lead type | Count | What the email asks for |
|---|---|---|
| Company with a known title shooting or in post | 18 | A call timed to *that* film's lock |
| Company with a slate but no confirmed single film | 22 | A call "when something on the slate is heading into post" |
| Company whose next step is development → pre-production | 5 | A conversation now, so the score is budgeted at prep, not after |

Anything that read as *development-only, no financing* was left out. So was
anything that looked like a student film, a micro-budget slasher with no music
line, or an unpaid "collaboration".

## 4. What is in every draft

Each draft is 150–190 words and contains exactly six things, in this order:

1. **A specific, accurate observation about a film they actually made** — a
   sonic problem the film had to solve, not a compliment.
2. **One line of identity**: composer and sound designer, Zazie Productions,
   the palette (waterphone, bowed metal, struck strings, custom synthesis).
3. **The reel**: `https://horror.zazieproductions.com` — placed high, no
   attachment, nothing to download.
4. **One concrete musical idea for their context** — the "pander to their
   desires" part, done as an idea rather than flattery: each is a method they
   could say yes or no to in ten seconds.
5. **The low-pressure call**: 15 minutes, "whenever you're getting close to
   picture lock", "no pitch deck, no pressure", "just reply whenever works".
6. **A P.S. that removes the size objection**: not the whole score — *one paid
   cue or one sequence* — plus an offer to work alongside an existing composer
   as additional support. Paid is stated explicitly so nothing reads as spec.

## 5. Sending plan (when this gets used)

Do not send 45 at once. Send in this order, ~8–10 a week, and keep the rest as
drafts so replies are manageable:

- **Week 1:** #25 Causeway, #1 Rooks Nest, #3 Divide/Conquer, #28 Frakas,
  #16 Kazak, #27 Warp, #24 Rhombus, #23 Scythia — the strongest genre fits.
- **Week 2:** the UK/Ireland group (#2, #10, #11, #12, #29, #33).
- **Week 3:** continental Europe (#5, #6, #13, #14, #15, #19, #20, #21, #35,
  #36, #37, #40, #41).
- **Week 4:** the rest (#4, #7, #8, #9, #17, #18, #22, #26, #30–#32, #34,
  #38, #39, #42–#45).

Follow-up: one line, one time, 12–14 days later — "still happy to send two
references if the timing is wrong" — then stop. No third touch.

Before sending any of them, re-check the company's current slate. Production
schedules move, and the draft's value is that it is specific; a stale reference
costs more than a late email.

## 6. Known limits

- The subject line and body are written for the named person; for the two
  team-inbox leads (#17, #42, #45) there is no named recipient, so the greeting
  is "Hello" — fix it if a name surfaces.
- Contact routes were verified on 2026-10-30 from company sites and official
  industry directories. Inboxes change; two bounces in a row is a signal to
  find a new route rather than resend.
- Credit references are limited to titles the company is publicly credited on.
  Where no safe title existed, the email argues from register and scale instead
  of naming a film — that is deliberate, not an omission.
- Nothing here is sent, scheduled, or tracked by automation. There is no
  tracking pixel, no link shortener, and no HTML template.
