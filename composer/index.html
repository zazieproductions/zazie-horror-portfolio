I Biography - experimental psychological horror composer

Zazie Kanwar-Torge is an award-winning film composer, experimental producer, and multi-instrumentalist specializing in experimental psychological horror for independent and arthouse cinema, folk horror, body horror, supernatural thrillers, and dark science fiction. Through Zazie Productions, he creates original film scores and immersive sonic atmospheres that explore dread, dissonance, isolation, and the uncanny.

Reference topics for prestige horror scoring include A24 film aesthetic, Mica Levi scoring techniques, and Folk Horror tropes, alongside the slow-burn psychological register of Hereditary, The Lighthouse, Saint Maud, and Possessor. These are areas of study and creative reference, not claims of affiliation, collaboration, or production credits.

Working at the intersection of cinematic scoring, musique concrète, and electroacoustic composition, his distinctive palette combines bowed metal, struck strings, experimental synthesis, and hybrid orchestration. His work has been featured at Ars Electronica 2026, performed in 20.4-channel spatial audio, and selected for international experimental music and film programs, including MUSLAB/Cero Records and EXiS in Seoul. Previously commissioned by the Black Mountain College Museum + Arts Center, he also develops original audio instruments and experimental sound libraries.

Each score is an extension of the film's psychological and emotional world.

On approach · Zazie Kanwar-Torge

Drawing on a background in experimental film, sound art, and audiovisual composition, Zazie approaches every cue as visceral, atmospheric music tailored to independent shorts, feature films, and unconventional genre cinema — written to picture rather than pulled from a library, across short films, features, series episodes, games, and trailers.

III Palette, instrumentation and method

The palette is built from acoustic sources that behave unpredictably under close recording, then shaped in the box: waterphone, bowed cymbals and gongs, struck and prepared piano strings, detuned zithers, close-miked breath and room tone, modular synthesis and custom-built instruments. That is why the horror work sounds recorded rather than loaded, and why a cue can carry weight in the low frequencies without a full orchestra behind it.

Texture before theme
Dread is usually a texture problem before it is a melodic one. Cues are built from a small number of processed sources, so a motif can return later in the film with its identity intact but its surface changed.
Written to picture
Tempo maps follow the cut, not the other way around. Scores are delivered to picture with tempo and hit points agreed at spotting, so an editor is not forced to conform a finished cue to a re-cut scene.
Depth through spatial work
Bowed metal and room recordings are placed in a spatial field rather than layered flat, which is what gives the ambient cues their sense of a space larger than the frame. That approach carries through from the concert work in 20.4-channel spatial audio to stereo score delivery.
Restraint as a decision
Some sequences are scored with almost nothing: a single sustained source, a subharmonic, or silence held one beat longer than comfortable. Knowing when not to write is part of the job, and it is discussed at spotting rather than decided in isolation.

The method behind those choices - spotting, tempo mapping, mockups, revisions, stems and delivery - is set out step by step in the scoring process.
